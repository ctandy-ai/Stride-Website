Stride Classification Card

Demand first, then a belt per attribute, then the ceiling and its modifier, availability, pathway and program. The card proposes from the numbers; the clinician confirms or overrides and signs.

Example athlete loaded (not a real client): AFL midfielder, sub-elite, 9 months after a left ACL reconstruction. Nothing about an athlete is saved on this device; press New athlete to start a real card, and Re-test to load the last card code from Cliniko.

RRe-test from the last card

Paste the card code from the bottom of the athlete's last Cliniko classification note. The demand profile fills in, test fields clear for the new numbers, and every attribute shows its previous belt so the change is visible. Nothing is stored here; the history stays in Cliniko.

1Demand profile

Always first. The sport decides which attributes are demanded and which running stream applies; the level selects the normative band each test is read against. A belt is a comparison against demand, so no demand profile, no belt.

Club training and games count toward the weekly high-speed running band. The Stride running target is the band minus what the athlete already runs outside.

2Belt per attribute

Enter what was tested. Each row shows the proposed belt and how it was reached: calculated from a locked threshold, or judgement where the normative source is not yet declared. Every gate reads the other limb, the sport and level, and the game's demand. Set the final belt; if it differs from the proposal, give the reason.

Asymmetry ladder: 7% or under clear; 7 to 10% hold (no promotion this block); over 10% drops one belt.
RSI: under 1.5 Learn, 1.5 to 2.0 Develop, over 2.0 Perform. HSR per week: 0 Restore, under 150 m Learn, 150 to 400 m Develop, 400 m and over Perform. Contacts per session: 20 or under Restore, 60 Learn, 120 Develop, over 120 Perform.

3Ceiling and modifier

The ceiling is the lowest belt among demanded attributes, capped by tissue stage. It caps game-like tasks only, never the whole session: isolated work runs at the belt of the attribute it loads.

4Availability

A separate axis, never merged into the belt. A failed psychological readiness screen is a hard hold whatever the belt says. Competition gate: 95% symmetry or better held in a fatigued state.

5Pathway, program and sign-off

The ceiling belt selects the pathway and the base programs. The demand profile selects the running stream. The gym program is set by the strength and power belts (isolated work runs at its own belt); the running program is set by the ceiling, because it carries the game-like work. File names match the v6 import files.

6The athlete's report

Built from the card: what they've built, where they're at in plain words, what it means in their sport, what they'll notice, the next steps and the re-test marker. The four fields below make it theirs. None of them is saved or put in the card code.

7Override log

Every override is saved without the athlete's identity: sport, level, attribute, proposed belt, final belt and reason. Over time this shows where the thresholds are wrong, which is how the gates get refined.
